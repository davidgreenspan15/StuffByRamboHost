Privacy policy

Last updated: September 28, 2026

Android Mirror sends your live phone screen directly to your paired headset. We do not receive the stream or operate a cloud relay.

Who this policy covers

This policy covers the Android Mirror Android companion and visionOS viewer developed by David (Rami) Greenspan. Contact rami@stuffbyrambo.com with privacy questions.

Live screen, audio, and control

When you approve sharing, the app captures your phone’s full display and sends it to your paired headset over an encrypted local TLS connection. Your screen may include messages, photos, account information, or other sensitive content you choose to display. Supported app playback audio can also be sent if you allow audio capture. The app uses playback capture, not a microphone source, and does not capture call audio.

When phone control is enabled and you approve it for the current session, the headset sends your taps, gestures, navigation requests, and typed text to the phone. Android’s AccessibilityService performs those actions. It does not retrieve the accessibility window hierarchy or read existing text-field contents. Text you enter in another app may be saved or sent by that app according to your actions and its own policies.

Media and pending commands are processed in bounded memory buffers for live playback and interaction. Android Mirror does not save screen or audio recordings to files, upload streams to us, or include advertising. Optional diagnostics are described below.

Pairing and local discovery

The apps save device identities and approved peer records locally so that a device you already paired can be recognized. The phone’s private identity key is held in AndroidKeyStore; approved headset public keys are stored in private app preferences. The headset’s identity and saved phone certificates and names are held in Keychain.

While available for discovery, the phone advertises a friendly name, protocol version, and certificate-derived device identifier on your local network. Other devices on that network may see these discovery details. They do not grant access to the screen: authentication and your sharing approval are still required.

Your choices

First pairing requires matching eight-digit codes and approval on both devices. Every new capture session requires phone-side approval and Android’s screen-sharing permission. The app requests the full display by default. Your phone must stay awake and unlocked; locking it stops sharing. Android Mirror cannot unlock it remotely.

You can stop sharing or disable control from the phone, pause control or disconnect on the headset, revoke Accessibility or audio permission in Android Settings, and forget saved peers. Forget removes that device’s trust record locally; use Forget on both devices to remove both saved peer entries. It does not delete your app’s own identity key. Headset Keychain records can persist independently of an app reinstall, so use Forget before uninstalling when you want to remove a saved peer.

Optional usage analytics and crash reports

Both choices start off and are optional: mirroring works without them. On Android, you can enable usage analytics to help us understand basic use of Android Mirror through Google Analytics for Firebase. This can include app interaction events, app and operating-system versions, device information, an app-instance identifier, and approximate region derived by Google from the connection. We do not use advertising IDs or personalized advertising. Usage analytics is not included in the visionOS app.

You can separately choose to share crash reports through Google Firebase Crashlytics. Reports can include exception messages, stack traces, app and operating-system versions, device information, and installation identifiers. If Android usage analytics is also enabled, recent app interaction events can appear as diagnostic breadcrumbs. Reports are submitted when you next open the app with crash reporting still enabled. We use them to investigate stability issues. We do not attach your screen, audio, typed text, pairing codes, trust keys, peer names, or other apps’ contents to diagnostics. A crash report is technical diagnostic data, not a screen recording.

You can turn these choices off in the app. Turning off crash reporting stops future report submissions and requests deletion of reports not submitted for sending. It cannot cancel a batch already submitted for sending or recall a report already delivered. Demo actions do not generate usage events. Crash reports from a run that ends in demo mode are excluded. Reports submitted before entering demo mode may finish sending. Google processes enabled diagnostics on our behalf; see Firebase privacy information. Crashlytics retains crash information and associated identifiers for 90 days before beginning removal. Analytics event and user data follow the service’s configured retention controls; aggregated reports may be retained longer. See Google’s Analytics retention information. Turning analytics off stops future analytics collection and resets analytics data held by the app; it does not by itself delete information already received by Google. Contact us with privacy or deletion requests without sending private screen content.

Local diagnostics and retention

The apps write limited operational diagnostics to the device’s operating-system logs, such as connection stages and error types. They are not intended to contain screen content, playback audio, typed text, pairing codes, or private keys, and the app does not upload these logs to us. The operating system controls local log retention.

Live buffers are discarded as they are processed or the session ends. Saved pairing records remain until you forget them or the relevant app storage is removed. This does not affect content retained by other apps you control through mirroring.

Contacting support

If you email support, we receive your email address and whatever information you choose to include. We use that correspondence to respond and investigate your request; your email provider and our mailbox provider process it as part of email delivery and storage. Do not send passwords, pairing codes, or private screen recordings. You can ask us to delete support correspondence at rami@stuffbyrambo.com; we may need to retain information necessary to resolve an ongoing request or meet applicable obligations.

Other services and updates

Your operating systems, app stores, and the apps whose content you mirror have their own privacy practices. This policy describes Android Mirror’s handling. We may update it when the product changes; the latest version will show its update date here.
